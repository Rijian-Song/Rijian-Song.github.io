chronic wound repair

HA matrices for MSCs and extracellular vesicles, with attention to local retention, inflammation, and tissue repair.

Engineering the local environment for repair

My work on chronic wounds asks how an injectable matrix can keep a therapeutic intervention at the wound site while supporting the biological processes needed for repair. The material, the delivered cargo, and the changing wound environment all contribute to the outcome.

Our published work on MSC-derived exosome delivery used an injectable HA hydrogel to address local retention, with preclinical evidence relating to wound closure, vascularisation, and re-epithelialisation. A complementary direction, presented at ESB 2026, investigates HA hydrogels as three-dimensional environments for living MSCs, including cell survival, spheroid formation, and paracrine activity.

Related work on a thiol-rich HA hydrogel examined oxidative stress, inflammation, and fibrosis in a rat model of intrauterine adhesion. Together, these studies motivate a broader question: how can material chemistry and local delivery support repair while limiting persistent inflammatory and fibrotic responses?

Questions guiding this direction

  • Retention and activity: How do mesh structure, degradation, and cargo–matrix interactions affect the duration of local exposure and the activity of retained cells or vesicles?
  • A changing microenvironment: How should mechanics and release respond to changes in oxidative stress, enzymes, and the stage of healing?
  • Quality of repair: How do vascularisation, epithelial restoration, inflammatory resolution, and collagen organisation relate to wound closure over time?

I want to connect these measurements to the timing of treatment. Recent work on feedback-responsive wound hydrogels provides a useful direction for designing materials whose behaviour changes with the local environment. Translation will require evidence for each formulation and cargo combination, with cell-based and cell-free approaches evaluated separately.
